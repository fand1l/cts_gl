Circle to Search

Shake the mouse. Circle anything on your screen. Get a Google Lens result.

Google’s phone gesture, for KDE Plasma 6 on Wayland.

Install it GitHub

Draw across this — the stroke follows your pointer exactly as it does in the program.

What it does

Shake the pointer diagonally — down-right, up-left, down-right — and the screen freezes and dims. Draw a loop around whatever you were curious about. It opens in Google Lens in your browser.

That is the whole idea. Everything below is what happens around it.

The gesture

Shake it diagonally

Shake the pointer diagonally — down-right, up-left, down-right — briskly, in one place. Roughly 150 pixels a swing, twice, inside about half a second.

If it does not fire, or fires while you are just working, do not go hunting through settings: Settings → Calibrate the gesture… measures a dozen of your own shakes and writes all the thresholds from them.

You can also press Meta + Shift + L, or use Capture now in the tray menu.

The dot below travels the exact movement the default thresholds ask for: two reversals, 150 px a swing, 700 px/s, 30° of tolerance from the diagonal. Those are the numbers the installer writes into kwinrc.

Features

What happens around the gesture

Circle it, or drag a box

Freehand by default, like on a phone. Shift for a rectangle, or click a window to take exactly that window.

A wide white line with a coloured glow that follows your hand. The glow takes its colour from where it is on the screen: blue at the top, red at about two fifths, yellow a little below that, green at the bottom.

Nothing is sent until you say so

The selection waits with handles you can drag and buttons that say what each one does. An upload cannot be taken back, so there is a moment to check first.

Above it, two lines: the size of what you have selected, and under it what will actually leave — → 1000 × 400, ~30 KB JPEG. They are not the same number.

Black out anything that must not leave

A token, an address, a name in the corner. It is filled solid before the image is made, so no version of it with them still in ever exists — not in the clipboard, not in the saved PNG, not in the page the browser posts.

The words under them are dropped from the recognised text too. Backspace undoes the last rectangle.

Select the text on your frozen screen

The words become selectable, like text in a browser. Search for the words themselves rather than a picture of them — or copy them, without any of it leaving the machine.

Searching here uploads nothing at all: the words are already read, so it is a plain web search. The reading runs tesseract on your own machine, and it is off until you ask for it.

Or pin it to the screen

The crop stays on your screen as a small window above everything else, which you can drag around, scroll to zoom, and close with Esc.

Useful whenever the thing you have to read is behind the window you have to type into. Ctrl + C copies it, and Ctrl with a drag carries the picture straight into a chat or a document.

Pick a colour off the screen

The dimming lifts, the magnifier follows your pointer with the pixel’s colour and hex under it, and a click copies #rrggbb — or rgb(…) with Shift held.

Plasma has an eyedropper of its own, so this is not new for the desktop. It is here because the answer was already on screen: this program had frozen the screen and magnified it under your pointer, and was throwing the value away.

Click a window

Instead of drawing carefully around it — it takes exactly that window.

A magnifier while you aim

So the edge lands where you meant.

Captures are kept, and you can search them

The box at the top searches the text that was recognised inside them, so “what was that error I looked up last week” is answerable.

Every capture can be found again, copied, saved or read from here. How many are kept is up to you.

  • A QR code wears its own button

    Any code on the frozen screen is read where it sits and wears a small button with the site it points at — press it and you are there. Decoded on your machine, in about a millisecond, and nothing about it is uploaded.

    Two codes on screen get two buttons, because one button cannot say which it means.

  • The same area as last time

    To the pixel — for watching a number that changes, where a rectangle redrawn by hand is never quite the same one.

    The same area on the bar, or the R key.

Privacy

Plainly

  • You can paint over part of it first. Black out fills the rectangles you drag solid, and it happens before the image is made — not on top of it. There is no copy of the picture with them still in, in the clipboard, in the saved PNG, in the kept capture or in the page the browser posts. The words under them are dropped from the recognised text too.
  • The daemon makes no network connections of its own. It writes a small local page with your selection inlined and hands it to your browser, and the browser is what talks to Google. So the session that uploads is your session, which is also why the result page opens where you can read it.
  • Text recognition is local. tesseract, on your machine, and only if you turn it on.
  • No API keys, no accounts, no telemetry. Nothing is collected.
  • The last five captures are kept in ~/.local/share/circle-to-search/recent so the tray can offer them again. Turn it off, or use ./uninstall.sh --purge.

This site too: not one external request. No fonts, no CDN, no analytics, no counters. Everything you see lives in the same repository.

Installing

Three commands

You need KDE Plasma 6 on Wayland and Python 3.11 or newer.

bash 
git clone https://github.com/fand1l/cts_gl.git
cd cts_gl
./install.sh

Log out and back in after installing, so KWin loads the script.

Who wrote this

This program was written by Claude

Anthropic’s AI model, over a series of sessions — the architecture, the code, the tests, and this documentation.

@fand1l directed the work: chose what to build, tested every version on real hardware, and sent back the screenshots and bug reports that corrected it. Several of the things that make it worth using — selectable text on the frozen screen, the Android-style stroke, catching the window that could not be seen — exist because they tried it and said what was wrong.

It seemed better to say so than to let you assume otherwise.
